## Key enterprise AI notetaker pricing

Enterprise AI notetaker pricing falls into three structural models. Understanding each helps you predict where your actual spend lands versus what appears on the pricing page.

### Enterprise per-user pricing

Seat-based pricing is the standard for enterprise tiers. You pay a fixed monthly fee per user regardless of meeting volume. This creates predictable billing, but the headline rate rarely includes everything.

### Per-recording or usage-based pricing

Some vendors layer usage limits on top of the seat fee. Fireflies caps AI credits at roughly 30-50 per user per month on paid tiers. Teams running a mix of sales calls, planning sessions, standups, and internal reviews weekly can exhaust those credits before the month ends. [Otter.ai](http://Otter.ai) applies monthly minute caps and per-conversation time limits across its plans, which can affect teams that conduct extended discovery sessions, all-hands calls, or high volumes of back-to-back meetings.

Flat per-seat pricing means no usage credits to track and no per-conversation time caps on paid plans. Granola charges [no per-meeting fees or credit limits](https://www.granola.ai/blog/granola-pricing-teams-per-user-enterprise), so the monthly bill stays predictable regardless of meeting volume.

Volume creates negotiating leverage. Enterprise contracts for seat-heavy deployments often include tiered discounts above 50 or 100 seats. Fireflies follows this pattern at scale. ## Budget traps: avoid common enterprise AI missteps

### Budgeting for AI notetaker training

Complex tools with multi-step onboarding require dedicated training time that isn't reflected in the vendor proposal. [Training and change management](https://www.vendr.com/blog/measuring-saas-tco-roi) can add meaningfully to first-year costs when adoption is low. Budget for facilitated onboarding sessions, internal documentation, and the productivity dip that typically follows any new tool rollout. Teams that skip this planning often find adoption stalls at the early-adopter stage, which means paying for seats that go unused and revisiting the procurement case six months later.

### Budgeting for integration costs

Most research team toolchains connect through Granola's native integrations at the Business tier, with no custom API work required. When teams build custom connections, maintenance costs compound quickly: plan for schema updates (changes to the data structure that break existing connections), OAuth lifecycle management (renewing and rotating authentication tokens that keep integrations authorized), and incident response when connections break. ## Key terms glossary

**Total Cost of Ownership (TCO):** A financial calculation covering lifecycle costs, including both direct costs like subscription fees and indirect costs like training time, integration overhead, and productivity impact.

**Single Sign-On (SSO):** An authentication method using one credential set across multiple independent software systems, simplifying enterprise user provisioning and reducing security risk from password sprawl.

**SOC 2 Type 2:** A security audit of operational controls evaluated over a six-to-twelve-month period, confirming that security practices function as intended and not just that they exist on paper.

**Seat-based pricing:** A SaaS billing model where you pay a fixed monthly fee per user regardless of usage volume, making costs predictable but requiring accurate seat count forecasting to avoid mid-contract overage negotiations.
