The short answer: Yes, but with three big caveats

Zoom's free Basic plan does support local8 recording. You do not need a paid license to capture a meeting as a video file. But Zoom's free plan limitations create three hard stops that the search results rarely surface upfront.

You need host permission

Recording on Zoom is a host-controlled feature. If you join a meeting as a participant, the record button is locked by default. The host has to grant you recording access explicitly, during the live meeting, before you can start.

Files only save locally9

Free plan recordings go directly to your computer's hard drive, not to Zoom's cloud. If your laptop crashes, you move the file to the wrong folder, or the conversion process is interrupted after the meeting, the recording can be corrupted and non-recoverable. There is no cloud backup because there is no cloud.

No AI summaries, no transcript, no cloud

Zoom's free tier does not include AI Companion. No automatic10 summaries, no transcription, no cloud backup. Rewatching a 45-minute video to find one key decision is the only option.

How to enable local11 recording on free Zoom

Before you can record, you need to turn on local12 recording in your account settings. This only works if you are the meeting host and requires the desktop client, not the browser version.

Step 1: Sign in to your Zoom account

Go to zoom.us and sign in. Open the desktop client and confirm your app is up to date before your next meeting. Zoom has a habit of surfacing an update prompt the moment a meeting starts, checking beforehand saves you that particular stress.

Link text → http://zoom.uszoom.us
Link URLhttp://zoom.us

Step 2: Enable local13 recording in settings

Navigate to the web portal, then Settings, then the Recording tab. Toggle "Record to computer files" to the on position. If the toggle appears grayed out, your organization's Zoom administrator has locked the setting at the account level, and you will need to contact them to unlock it. Zoom's local14 recording documentation confirms the exact navigation path. The grayed-out toggle is where a lot of people hit a wall. If that's you, the IT request is the actual Step 2.

Link URLhttps://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0063423

Step 3: Start recording during a meeting

Once local16 recording is enabled and you are hosting, click the Record button at the bottom of the Zoom window. A red dot labeled "Recording" appears in the upper left corner, visible to all participants. You can pause or stop the recording from the same control bar. Worth knowing: that red dot is visible to everyone in the call the moment you hit record, which changes the room more than most people expect.17

Step 4: Find your recording after the meeting ends

After you end the meeting, Zoom begins converting the recording file. Do not close your laptop or restart your computer during this process. According to GVSU's Zoom documentation, if the conversion is interrupted by sleep mode, a restart, or a crash, recording files can become corrupted and non-recoverable. Once conversion completes, the folder containing your files opens automatically18. The conversion wait is where most people give up, close the laptop, interrupt the process, and a week later you're wondering where that meeting went.

The host-permission gotcha most people miss

The recording permission requirement stops most free Zoom users cold. Understanding why it exists changes how you approach meeting documentation entirely.

Why participants can't record without permission

Zoom blocks participants from recording by default as a consent mechanism. The platform assumes not everyone in a meeting has agreed to be recorded, so it gives the host control over who can capture the conversation.

Anyone running a conversation where accurate documentation matters (investor pitches, M&A discussions, confidential hiring calls) faces a genuine problem. Asking "can you give me recording permission?" mid-conversation interrupts the flow and shifts the dynamic of the meeting.19

How to request recording permission

If you are a participant who needs to record, the host has to open the Participants panel, hover over your name, click "More," and select "Allow Record." This happens in real time during the meeting and requires the host to take manual action for each participant who needs access.

In practice, this means you have to ask, the host has to remember, and both of you have to pull attention away from the conversation to make it happen.20

What happens when the host says no

If the host declines or forgets to grant permission, you have no recording option within Zoom on the free plan. Your only path forward is manual notes taken during the meeting, a solution that forces you to split attention between the conversation and your keyboard. For confidential conversations where you need accurate documentation, this is a meaningful limitation.

Where free Zoom recordings actually save, and why you'll lose them

Local21 recording solves the "Did we capture it?" question but creates a new one: "Where did it go?"

Default save location on your computer

Zoom drops local22 recordings into a default folder based on your operating system. According to the Zoom community documentation:

Link text → https://community.zoom.com/t5/Zoom-Meetings/Where-are-my-recordings-78887/m-p/337872Zoom community documentation
Link URLhttps://community.zoom.com/t5/Zoom-Meetings/Where-are-my-recordings-78887/m-p/337872
  • Windows: C:\Users\[Username]\Documents\Zoom
  • Mac: /Users/[Username]/Documents/Zoom
  • Linux: home/[Username]/Documents/Zoom

Each meeting gets its own subfolder organized by date. Inside, you will find an .mp4 video file and an .m4a audio file.

Why local23 files are easy to lose

Zoom organizes folders by date, not by searchable content. Finding what was said in a specific meeting requires either remembering the date or scrubbing through video. Video files also eat storage fast: A 40-minute group call can produce several hundred megabytes to over a gigabyte depending on resolution, participant count, and whether screen sharing is active.

Files pile up quickly. Many users lose recordings simply by not knowing where to look after conversion completes.

No automatic24 backup or sync

Zoom's free plan does not automatically25 upload local26 recordings to Google Drive, Dropbox, or any cloud service. If you want the file backed up, you move it manually. For back-to-back meeting days, this creates a backlog of large video files that most people never get around to organizing.

When to use Zoom's free recording vs device-audio capture vs upgrading

No single approach fits every situation. Choose based on your role, the meeting type, and whether you need a video file or searchable notes.

Recording method decision checklist:

  • I am the meeting host
  • I need a raw video file with screen share content
  • No sensitive or confidential content is being discussed
  • I do not need searchable text or AI summaries
  • The 40-minute group meeting limit will not affect this call

If all five boxes apply, Zoom free local50 recording covers your needs.

Use Zoom free recording when you're the host

Free local51 recording works well when you are hosting, the content is not sensitive, and a video file is the goal. Training sessions, product demos, or team stand-ups where a raw video record is the deliverable fit this pattern. Keep group sessions under 40 minutes to stay within the Zoom free plan limit.

Use device-audio capture when you're a participant

Device-audio capture fits back-to-back meeting days, confidential conversations, and any situation where asking for recording permission would be awkward.52 It works across any meeting platform: Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Slack huddles, FaceTime, and WhatsApp. You get structured, searchable notes rather than a large video file to manage.

Upgrade to Zoom Pro when you need cloud storage

Zoom Pro makes sense when your organization needs centralized video storage, shared access to recordings across a team, or the AI Companion features for summaries and smart chapters. The 10 GB cloud storage and shareable links address use cases where a video record specifically matters. For teams focused on capturing decisions and action items, device-audio capture typically covers more ground at lower cost. Pricing is subject to change, so verify current rates on Zoom's official pricing page.

FAQs

How long can I record on free Zoom?

Group meetings with three or more participants cut off automatically54 at 40 minutes on the free plan, per Zoom's free plan documentation. According to third-party sources, one-on-one meetings between exactly two participants are not subject to the 40-minute limit, though Zoom's official documentation suggests the limit applies more broadly. Verify current limits before relying on extended 1:1 meetings. Zoom's overall 30-hour meeting duration cap applies on paid plans.

Can I get a transcript from a free Zoom recording?

Free Zoom does not generate a transcript from local55 recordings. Cloud transcription requires a paid Zoom plan with cloud recording enabled. Third-party device-audio tools like Granola provide automatic56 transcription without requiring a Zoom upgrade, and the Granola pricing overview covers what is included on each plan.

What happens to my recording if Zoom crashes?

If Zoom crashes or the post-meeting conversion is interrupted, recording files can become corrupted and non-recoverable. Restarting your computer, closing the laptop lid, or losing power during conversion are all common causes. There is no cloud backup for free plan recordings, so the loss is permanent. Granola's device-audio approach removes this risk because the transcript generates in real time and syncs to the app, rather than depending on a local conversion process.57,58
