# Granola SEO review — brief for the agency

Brand-safety and liability review. Not a copy edit. You are looking for two failures only.

**1. Copy that sells Granola on other people not knowing it's there.**
**2. Copy that claims notes stay off Granola's servers.** They don't — notes and transcripts are cloud-stored and sync to our servers.

A miss is expensive, a false positive is cheap. When borderline, flag it.

---

## Flag these

**Imperceptibility sold as a benefit**
"No bot joins your call", "bot-free", "no visible participant", "nothing appears in the attendee list". Describing how capture works is fine. Making invisibility the reason to buy is not.

**Claims that a bot changes what people say**
This is the most serious one. "People speak more freely without a bot in the room", "a bot changes the tone", "announcing recording makes people guard what they say", "counterparties see a named bot and hold back". The argument reduces to: *participants will say things they wouldn't say if they knew*. Never make it.

**High-stakes conversations used as the setting for that argument**
Performance reviews, terminations, restructuring, layoffs, board and investor conversations, legal, medical, therapy-adjacent. Granola may be mentioned as useful in serious meetings. It must never be positioned as the tool that gets candour *because* it isn't noticed. Treat any pairing of a sensitive context with an argument about disclosure, bots or perception as flag-worthy.

**What participants notice or feel**
"Without interrupting the flow", "no awkward announcement", "participants stay comfortable". Comparison tables are the worst offender — rows headed *Participant visibility* or *Participant comfort* make perceptibility the buying criterion.

**Running down meeting bots or their users**
"Disruptive", "intrusive", "kills the conversation", "generic summaries", "reduces presence".

**Untrue storage claims**
"Never leaves your Mac", "no cloud", "not on a third-party server", and unscoped absolutes — "no storage", "nothing is stored anywhere" — which readers apply to their notes.

---

## Do not flag these

- **Audio capture and deletion.** "Captures your laptop's audio", "the recording is deleted once notes are generated", "no recording is stored". All accurate.
- **Security and compliance as fact.** SOC 2, GDPR, encryption, SSO, admin controls. "Secure" and "compliant" are procurement words, not warnings.
- **Factual descriptions of how capture works**, where invisibility isn't the payoff.

---

## Writing the fix

Take a bigger position than the sentence. If a paragraph's point rests on nobody noticing, cut the point — don't soften the wording.

Write toward what Granola does: better notes, staying present, less admin. Where disclosure is relevant, lean into it — we offer an in-chat notification and a video watermark. Never introduce the no-bot fact into copy that doesn't already have it.

Rewrite more often than you delete. Deletion suits body prose. Headings, table cells, blockquotes and meta descriptions need replacing, not removing, or you leave a hole. Check your replacement reads correctly against the sentences either side — the commonest defect is a fix that duplicates neighbouring copy.

## Your remit

Every article is labelled.

| Label | What you do |
|---|---|
| **Review & decide** | Yours to finish. Accept, delete or dismiss. |
| **Review & recommend** | Review everything, leave a note saying what you'd do. Don't accept or delete. |
| **Do not review** | Consent, privacy, storage, security, competitor comparisons. Granola handles these. |

Red findings are reliable — roughly 19 in 20 get actioned. Amber is noisier, about 3 in 5, and needs judgement.

Escalate rather than decide whenever a finding turns on participant awareness, on what a bot changes about a conversation, or on where notes are stored — including on "Review & decide" articles.
